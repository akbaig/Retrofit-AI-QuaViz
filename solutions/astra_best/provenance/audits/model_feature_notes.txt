Actual learned-model feature audit: extra_direct_01
==================================================

Scope
-----
Read model.joblib, training.json, policy.json, the synthetic DEVELOPMENT feature
cache, its labels and its stored probabilities. No holdout/test data or original
validation data were read. Analysis used the existing model without refitting or
changing pipeline code. Development contains 7 assets, 30,240 minutes and 21
label records (19 observable). It has no observable BEARING_WEAR or MISALIGNMENT
examples, so it cannot establish detection quality for those classes.

Actual architecture, not the initially proposed architecture
----------------------------------------------------------
This run has 13 independent binary ExtraTrees classifiers, each 180 trees,
max_depth=24, min_samples_leaf=4, max_features=.65. Its 343 inputs combine
rating-scaled sensor levels, causal 10/60-minute means/spans/deltas, data freshness
and flat-run durations, operating context, static ratings/profile/control,
48 explicit physics features (plus selected trailing transforms), and 13
observed-waveform features plus age. It supports concurrent faults. Missing
values become the distinct -999 code at the classifier boundary.

Training includes original_train + synthetic train + synthetic train_balanced:
247,680 minutes from 58 assets, with event-balanced positive sampling and 18,000
negative examples per fault. This run DISABLES learned healthy-reference
regressors and learned residuals (include_residuals=false). It must not be
described as a conditional healthy-regressor system. Its selected global policy
uses probability .55, 5-minute persistence, 60-minute recovery, 24-hour reminders.

What the classifiers actually use
--------------------------------
BEARING_WEAR: mean10/mean60 vibration crest together 37.32%; delta60 motor speed
12.72%; maximum 60-minute noise level 12.44%; snapshot BPFO envelope prominence
6.28%; mean10/mean60 acceleration together 8.71%. This is a crest/acceleration
classifier with meaningful but secondary BPFO corroboration. Development has no
observable bearing positives; it cannot validate claimed early bearing recall.

MISALIGNMENT: current/speed^3 9.98%; rated pump efficiency 7.21%; mean10 velocity
6.73%; normalized speed 5.51%; mean60 bearing thermal residual 4.65%; motor rating
3.89%. The expected 2x/3x waveform signature is barely used (all snapshots 0.30%).
No development misalignment positives exist. Misalignment probability reaches
median .629 during one development imbalance event, so cross-mechanical confusion
is already visible.

IMBALANCE: valve-demand residual 20.18%; vibration freshness 9.71%; mean10/60
velocity 9.52%; speed-normalized vibration 4.01%; pump head rating 3.83%; bearing
freshness 3.29%; static head rating 3.21%. Valve residual is present primarily for
VFD_PRESSURE equipment: this is a suspicious control/instrument shortcut for a
general rotor fault, not a credible physical cause of imbalance.

SUCTION_STRAINER_CLOGGING: INDUSTRIAL_CW profile 16.05%; 60-minute change in curve
residual 5.11%; mean60 speed-command residual 4.26%; suction pressure 4.04%;
discharge pressure 3.74%; normalized raw discharge 3.61%. Some physics is useful,
but profile dependence is large. Both development clogging events stay below
.55 despite measured suction decline on the instrumented pump.

CAVITATION: FLOW_VALVE control 16.78%; rated efficiency 15.27%; PROCESS_TRANSFER
profile 11.70%; motor rating 9.69%; supply temperature 5.36%; speed-command
residual 5.09%. Static attributes dominate actual NPSH evidence. The development
VFD_PRESSURE cavitation has estimated NPSH margin median 1.115, yet its median
class probability is .016. Positive/negative ranking is excellent (AUC/AP 1.0)
over just 37 eligible positive minutes, but the global .55 threshold detects none.

DISCHARGE_RESTRICTION: current/speed^3 4.50%; rated flow 4.37%; mean10 discharge
4.05%; normalized discharge 3.95%; head rating 3.82%; raw normalized discharge
3.65%. Development separation is relatively good (AP .801, AUC .958), although
restriction on the uninstrumented LT pump is often confused with impeller wear.

IMPELLER_WEAR: motor-power age ALONE 40.61%, followed by motor rating 2.93%,
current/speed^3 2.88%, electrical power factor 2.88%, motor-power range 2.87%,
10-minute power span 2.87%. The model often calls the missing-power legacy pump
impeller wear, while missing the instrumented seawater wear event. The strong
feature is a missing-instrument shortcut, not a measured pump-curve deficit.

AIR_ENTRAINMENT: INDUSTRIAL_CW profile 10.28%; head/speed^2 8.71%; 60-minute suction
delta 6.32%; inferred flow requirement residual 5.01%; supply temperature 4.98%;
efficiency rating 4.88%; power/speed^3 4.81%. Development seawater air entrainment
never crosses .55, while air is incorrectly favored during seawater impeller wear.

SENSOR_DRIFT: supply temperature 7.39%; pump head rating 6.58%; flow rating 5.35%;
motor rating 5.16%; static head 4.36%; bearing thermal residual 3.17%. The visible
development fault affects MOTOR POWER, but the model is mostly using context and
asset metadata. AP .0088, AUC .522; median positive probability .075.

SENSOR_CALIBRATION_SHIFT: head rating 3.76%; static head 3.56%; bearing thermal
residual 3.49%; motor rating 3.10%; 60-minute power delta 2.87%; discharge noise
2.20%. Both development shifts (power and fluid temperature) are missed. AP .0087,
AUC .332. Small lasting offsets need a learned healthy expectation or temporal
change evidence; absolute profiles and short post-step deltas do not suffice.

SENSOR_NOISE_SPIKES: mean60/mean10 discharge-pressure std together 14.38%; head
rating 6.54%; motor rating 5.98%; static head 4.46%; supply temperature 4.16%; flow
rating 3.78%; discharge range 3.49%; cross-channel maximum mean60 std 3.15%.
Development noise affects CURRENT, CREST, and ACCELERATION. All three are missed
by the .55 policy. AP .0485, AUC .483. This is a tag-transfer blind spot, not lack
of detectable noise: current stdmean60 rises .0092 -> .0421, and acceleration
stdmean60 rises .0113 -> .0332. The global maximum can also be dominated by an
unrelated naturally noisy channel, masking the changing channel.

SENSOR_STUCK: maximum flat duration 47.21% plus maximum repeated measured updates
32.40%. These are generic cross-tag evidence. The discharge-pressure stuck event
has median predicted probability 1.0, AP/AUC 1.0, and 100% of eligible positives
above .55. Counts refer to newly measured equal readings, not sparse held values.

COMMUNICATION_LOSS: fluid valid-sample count 45.01%; fluid range 7.30%; fluid level
6.09%; fluid relative noise 6.01%; vibration level 5.55%; vibration alarm fraction
5.52%; fresh fluid 5.15%. Both development gateway outages are detected with
median probability 1.0. AP .999985 and AUC .999999. It recognizes observed data
absence well, although reliance on particular channel counts deserves sensor-drop
versus whole-gateway coverage in future tests.

Observed waveform opportunity and ablation rationale
---------------------------------------------------
In development asset 07, before versus during the imbalance, median measured
1x velocity rises 0.205 -> 1.220 mm/s, while 2x/1x falls .849 -> .129. Velocity
RMS rises .747 -> 1.541 mm/s and speed-normalized velocity rises 2.032 -> 4.191.
The waveform is available for 93.4% of active steady minutes, yet median imbalance
probability is .035. This is direct evidence that the physical feature exists but
this fit has not learned to depend on it. Snapshot importance is currently below
1% for both imbalance and misalignment, versus 7.19% for bearing (mostly BPFO).

Conversely, snapshot importance 8.41% for SENSOR_NOISE_SPIKES cannot be explained
as direct monitoring-sensor corruption evidence: simulator waveforms are generated
from physical vibration, separately from telemetry sensor corruptions. That may
represent useful corroboration, but it may also be an incidental training-domain
association. Do not claim waveform ablation will improve every class a priori.

Generic improvement directions supported by this audit
------------------------------------------------------
1. Refit without explicit profile/rating shortcuts and with controlled exposure
   to missingness patterns; retain engineering ratings inside normalization.
   Simply dropping asset_id is insufficient to prevent asset-family shortcuts.
2. Train each machine diagnosis on physically relevant feature groups plus enough
   counterexamples across all instrument/control profiles. Mechanical classes
   should see speed-normalized orders, envelope evidence and thermal residuals.
3. Add shared per-channel sensor-fault features normalized to expected healthy
   noise and context, then pool channel-level evidence. A discharge-only noise
   training distribution does not transfer to acceleration/current failures.
4. Restore learned healthy expected-value residuals and test their contribution,
   especially for drift, calibration offsets and low-flow hydraulic faults.
5. Use per-class development calibration/threshold selection only with adequate
   class coverage. Cavitation ranking is promising but scores are not calibrated;
   bearing/misalignment are absent from this small development sample.

These are diagnosis of this specific fit, not evidence that any proposed change
will improve the final withheld benchmark. No model or policy was changed here.
